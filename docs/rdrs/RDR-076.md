## Problem Statement

Deterministic checks within two representations do not establish correspondence
between them. A TLA+ model can satisfy its checked properties while its code
implementation passes unit tests, yet the two can disagree about ownership,
ordering, retries, or failure behavior. Both may also encode the same mistaken
interpretation of business intent.

The originating problem was identifying and correcting drift between formal
models and implementation. LID specifications and tests were proposed as
intermediate representations that could narrow the correspondence gaps. The
broader requirement is to establish and maintain correspondence between approved
business intent and implemented behavior, using the checks appropriate to each
project. Formal models and LID are supported, optional project capabilities.

Paid should expose which obligations have support, what that support actually
establishes, where representations disagree, and what remains uncertain. It
should propose coordinated repairs without treating observed code, linked
artifacts, or passing checks as authority for intended behavior.

## Context

### Confirmed scope

The add-on is hosted by Paid and reuses its execution, knowledge, authorization,
and review infrastructure. Its primary unit is a scoped business obligation,
not a PR or tool invocation. A PR can supply evidence or carry an authorized
repair, but PR readiness is not the add-on's defining purpose.

An obligation is a claim about required behavior, with source references,
interpretation, scope, and approval state. A correspondence claim explains how
one representation implements or expresses another, including abstraction
boundaries and assumptions. A check result is evidence about a declared claim
and scope; it is not unrestricted proof of correctness.

## Research Findings

Repository evidence supports reuse, with important limits:

| Existing capability | Evidence | Reuse and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Durable user direction | `docs/intent/change-intent-records/`; RDR-042 | CIR approval and supersession exist. Their current capture heuristic targets non-obvious constraints and rejected alternatives; complete obligation capture must not depend on that heuristic. |
| Repository knowledge | `docs/intent/knowledge-base/`; `Knowledge::Collectors::OkfCollector` | OKF-compatible Markdown is indexed as curated knowledge. Indexing alone establishes neither approval nor authority. |
| Knowledge portability | `Knowledge::Okf::Export` | Can export change-intent artifacts as OKF-compatible files. Export is a snapshot, not repository synchronization or canonical-source transfer. |
| Linked intent | `docs/high-level-design.md`; `docs/lid/workflow.md` | LID organizes intent, designs, EARS, tests, and code; use the project's records rather than create parallel intent. |
| Execution and evidence | `AgentRuns::Verification`; `AgentRuns::VerificationResultRecorder`; `ArtifactStorage` | Existing execution/results can contribute evidence but do not establish cross-representation correspondence by themselves. |
| Independent code review | `Reviews::Verification::Pipeline` | Reuse bounded review, pinned revisions, and evidence validation where applicable; semantic correspondence needs its own scoped claims. |
| Approval and conformance | `app/services/feature_intents/`; `app/services/intent_conformance/` | Compose with existing approval and merge policies; do not replace or bypass them. |

The original brief supplies nine complementary capabilities and concrete rubric
examples. Risk categories and layers are separate dimensions. Neither replacing
six risk categories with nine layers nor counting successful checks demonstrates
sufficiency. No customer measurements or universal correctness claim are assumed.

## Proposed Solution

### 1. Authority, representation, and durable intent

Authority comes from an authorized user's decision and approval within an
explicit scope, not from an artifact's notation, recency, or level of detail.
Approved business intent governs intended behavior. Users may establish a model,
specification, or another artifact as authoritative for a particular property,
revision, and scope. A model's checked correspondence with approved business
intent supports that designation; model-checker success alone does not.

Keep requirements, proposals, safeguards, assumptions, observed behavior, and
unresolved interpretations distinct. Suspected errors in approved intent become
clarification requests with evidence; they do not authorize silent overrides.
Conflicting approved sources remain unresolved until the responsible user
establishes precedence or revises the intent. Code and characterization tests
show what happens, not what is authorized to happen.

Use the project's established intent framework as the durable home of decisions:

- In LID projects, update the owning intent records and cascade through affected
  designs, EARS, models, tests, and code. Some specifications are themselves
  approved intent; others are derived representations. Record that distinction.
- Honor other established intent frameworks and repository conventions.
- In projects already using OKF, propose scoped intent/CIR records in that bundle.
- Otherwise offer lightweight CIR records in OKF-compatible repository Markdown,
  and explicitly mention LID as an available, supported structured option.
  Adoption requires user approval and does not require adopting all of LID.

Repository intent records need stable identity, intended behavior, scope,
source/decision references, revision, approval evidence, unresolved questions,
and supersession references. CIR content captures rationale, constraints, and
rejected alternatives where relevant. Record required behavior even when it is
obvious or no alternative was rejected; CIR noise-reduction heuristics must not
hide an obligation or a user clarification needed for future verification.

A clarification is recorded and approved in the canonical intent record before
it authorizes downstream repair. A linked CIR may explain a LID decision without
becoming a second maintained definition of the same behavior. Paid's evidence
store references those records and revisions; it does not invent competing
intent. Repository persistence, approval provenance, and any Paid-side mirrors
need an explicit canonical-source contract in the LLD. Existing OKF export must
not be presented as that completed contract.

### 2. Entry paths and operation boundaries

| Entry path | Starting point | Intended result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design new behavior | Business sources and proposed behavior | Reviewed intent, an appropriate verification plan, and later implementation evidence. |
| Audit documented behavior | Business sources, code, specs, tests, and optional models | Source-grounded findings and current coverage within a bounded slice. This ships first. |
| Bootstrap existing behavior | Business sources and code with sparse engineering records | Separate reconstructed intent from observed behavior; propose reviewed intent records and incremental checks. |

One project can use different paths for different obligations. All paths share
obligation identity, authority references, check contracts, and coverage views.

**Audit and repair are separate operations.** An audit request authorizes
investigation, isolated check execution, evidence recording, reports, draft
clarifications, and repair proposals within its scope. It does not authorize
changes to canonical intent, models, specs, tests, or production code, or launch
repair work automatically. Audit fixtures and check artifacts remain isolated.
Approving an intent clarification and requesting a repair are distinct actions.

A separately requested repair may correct clear downstream discrepancies through
Paid's normal review process. It asks the user before changing approved meaning,
choosing among plausible interpretations, or resolving competing authorities.
A repair proposal names affected artifacts, source support, behavioral effects,
required decisions, and the checks that will verify the correction.

### User experience: Inbox decisions and project audit history

The Inbox is the primary surface for human-actionable verification findings and
decisions. Extend its existing queue/detail and contextual-chat patterns rather
than introduce a separate decision dashboard. Project and agent-run pages expose
audit history, progress, and both report views; routine progress and clean audit
reports do not create Inbox work.

An Inbox item identifies the project, obligation, scope, finding, and required
owner action. Its detail shows conflicting source passages or model/spec claims,
observed implementation behavior, check outcomes and limitations, freshness,
why a decision is needed, and possible resolutions with their consequences.
Evidence, audit runs, and canonical intent revisions remain directly accessible.

| Situation | Actions and outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Ambiguous intent or competing authority | Discuss in item-scoped chat; propose a clarification; review and approve the canonical intent-record change. Approval records interpretation, revision, scope, and decision authority. |
| Established implementation discrepancy | Inspect evidence; request a bounded repair for selected findings; or defer with a recorded reason. Repair starts only through the separate authorized action. |
| Missing/stale evidence or execution failure | Provide inputs, correct configuration, or request a bounded check rerun. A rerun is investigation, not permission to change maintained artifacts. |
| Repair awaiting review | Reuse Paid's existing plan, test, and PR-review Inbox flows, linked back to the originating finding and repair run. |

Chat exploration, acknowledgment, and deferral do not resolve a discrepancy or
approve intent. Approval of a proposed intent change is distinct from repair
authorization. Repository changes needed to record an approved clarification
follow the project's normal review/persistence process; dependent repair cannot
use that clarification as canonical until its required approval and persistence
complete. Show that intermediate state rather than silently treating chat as
recorded intent.

Repeated audits attach new observations to the same unresolved scoped finding
when its identity and cause persist. Distinct conflicts remain distinct;
textual similarity alone is not enough to merge them. Deferral remains visible
in findings/history with a reason and a route back to action. Only a recorded
authoritative resolution with the required fresh evidence resolves a finding;
an authorized exception records its scope without rewriting contradictory
check outcomes. New evidence or invalidated decisions can return work to the
Inbox. Actions revalidate current revisions and permissions, preventing stale
approvals and duplicate repair launches.

An audit can complete with findings while Inbox decisions remain open. Where
clarification is needed to interpret a particular result, that obligation stays
unresolved or its dependent checks wait; independent checks may continue. Run
completion, decision completion, and obligation satisfaction are separate states.
The UI and project-scoped chat/MCP offer equivalent authorized actions and links
through the same underlying policy boundary.

### Run initiation and optional automation

Audits and repairs are separate, linked agent runs using Paid's existing queue,
capacity, budgets, isolated execution, logs, and cancellation. They are not new
standalone agent services. A run can coordinate multiple selected checks without
requiring one run per layer. Specific goal names and lifecycle wiring belong in
the LLD.

| Trigger | Scope and execution | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual audit | Start from a project action or project-scoped chat/MCP request. Select the business slice, source references, target revision, and budget; create an audit run without requiring a GitHub issue. Show the resolved scope before dispatch and link to progress and the report. | First release. |
| Requested repair | From selected findings, review the proposed artifacts, behavioral effects, scope, unresolved decisions, and budget, then explicitly request repair. Create a separate run linked to the findings and originating audit; reuse normal plan/test/PR review. | Repair phase. |
| PR-triggered audit | Explicit project opt-in requests a focused audit of obligations affected by the PR revision. Create a linked audit run, reuse valid evidence, and leave uncertainty explicit. Do not add full layered audits to every PR or create a new merge gate by default. | Optional later integration. |
| Scheduled audit | Explicitly configure a recurring bounded audit through RDR-075 maintenance scheduling, including sources/scope, budgets, overlap prevention, and pause/disable controls. It invokes the same audit operation and surfaces actionable findings in the Inbox. | Optional later integration owned by the maintenance scheduler. |

Automatic triggers never grant repair authority. Overlapping requests must not
launch duplicate work for the same audit scope/revision or the same authorized
repair. A completed audit or approved clarification must not call the existing
automatic follow-up path to start a repair. Users can inspect trigger provenance,
current scope, waiting reasons, and links between runs, findings, decisions, and
PRs. Cancelling a run preserves evidence and unresolved findings without
reporting success. Optional automation is not required for core RDR closeout.

### 3. Coordination and layer capabilities

Keep three responsibilities separate: coordinate work, execute selected layer
capabilities, and synthesize coverage by obligation. These are responsibilities,
not a requirement for three services or one agent per layer. One run can perform
several capabilities; independent challenge uses a separate review context when
risk or uncertainty warrants it.

Rails owns identity, access, policy enforcement, budgets, revision validation,
scheduling, and state. Semantic interpretation, correspondence assessment, and
selection rationale use bounded agent judgment through `agent_harness` and human
review. Do not hard-code semantic risk classification or correctness scoring.

| Layer capability | Contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Business-process formalization | Source-linked workflows, rules, scenarios, assumptions, and open questions for business validation. |
| Verification planning | Per-obligation selection, rationale, omissions, dependencies, costs, and limitations. |
| Engineering translation | Derived specifications, model slices, tests, and implementation mappings; actual repairs require separate authorization. |
| Linkage and evidence freshness | Validate references, revisions, dependency changes, and unmapped material within declared scope. |
| Behavioral testing and execution coverage | Examples, boundaries, properties, integrations, and exercised behavior. |
| Formal verification | Checked properties, assumptions, bounds, counterexamples, and incomplete outcomes for projects using models. |
| Mutation testing | Deliberate faults, detection results, survivors, and limitations of the mutation set. |
| Implementation/specification conformance | Correspondence between implementation behavior and selected specifications or model abstractions. |
| Independent semantic audit | Source-grounded omissions, contradictions, unjustified assumptions, and shared interpretive blind spots. |

Each selected check declares inputs and revisions, obligation/correspondence
claims, preconditions, access needs, execution method, expected observations,
producer identity, cost bounds, result structure, limitations, and invalidation
dependencies. Layer selection does not imply a tool is available or ran.
Documented commands are acceptable integrations; a dedicated adapter is not a
prerequisite. A missing required tool produces an explicit gap.

### 4. Verification-selection rubric

Select for failure modes, available artifacts, evidence quality, and cost.
There is no minimum layer count, universal formal-method requirement, or
unconditional semantic-audit sign-off. Independent challenge is selected when
risk, ambiguity, contradictory evidence, or shared assumptions warrant it.

| Behavior or risk | Candidate checks | Selection guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligibility, thresholds, calculations, matching | Decision tables, boundary/property tests, mutation testing | Include ambiguity, false matches, and negative cases. |
| Concurrency, retries, ownership, partial completion | Formal checking when applicable, fault scenarios, protocol mutations, correspondence review | Make atomicity, fairness, acknowledgments, and failure assumptions explicit. |
| Parsing and normalization | Synthetic fixtures, generated inputs, boundary tests, mutation testing | Exercise malformed and ambiguous inputs; models may add little. |
| External contracts and persistence | Integration/contract tests, assumption evidence | A local model does not prove a remote service guarantee. |
| Human approvals and handoffs | Business walkthroughs, scenarios, process/audit evidence | Distinguish software enforcement from human conduct. |
| Mechanical implementation changes | Regression checks, linkage checks, focused correspondence review | Preserve a valid abstraction without forcing unnecessary model edits. |

These are reusable examples, not an exhaustive taxonomy or fixed scoring table.
Each plan states risk, selected checks, fit, exclusions/non-applicability,
required inputs, assumptions, resource expectations, and residual gaps.
Materially different failure modes should receive appropriately different plans;
minor input differences need not force different layer sets.

CI, security, branch protection, code review, and required approved-intent
conformance remain mandatory host policies, enforced independently. An agent
cannot waive them to satisfy an audit budget. Authorized host exceptions remain
explicit and separate; missing or failed host gates cannot be reported as
passing or bypassed by an obligation coverage result.

### 5. Complementary business-review views

Derive both views from shared records rather than maintain duplicate narratives:

1. **Intent validation:** actors, triggers, handoffs, decisions, approvals,
   ordering, completion, exceptional paths, recovery, concrete scenarios, and
   source passages. The business owner approves a specific interpretation,
   revision, and scope or records unresolved questions.
2. **Verification coverage:** selected checks and rationale, evidence and
   provenance, assumptions, conflicts, freshness, omissions, cost, findings,
   owners, and next actions, organized by obligation.

Approval of intended behavior does not certify implementation conformance.
Passing implementation checks does not approve intended behavior. Business
review must work through examples and exceptions without requiring source-code
or formal-notation expertise. Diagrams are optional explanatory views, not
proof or independently maintained definitions.

A review package binds obligation/source revisions, scope, plan revision,
evidence index, approvals, findings, and review decisions to a reproducible
package identity. Later changes create a new review basis and retain the prior
record. Package approval does not authorize repair or release unless the user
explicitly performs the corresponding operation under host policy.

### 6. Evidence, outcomes, and coverage

Maintain stable references to obligations and source passages, derived
representations, correspondence mappings, check inputs, producers, versions,
configuration, environment, results, artifacts, and limitations. Record random
seeds or replay data where relevant. Content digests identify inputs but do not
prove that dependency selection is complete or semantically correct.

Keep three dimensions separate:

- **Intent validation:** proposed, approved within scope, or unresolved.
- **Check execution and freshness:** completed, failed assertion, execution
  error, incomplete, not run, non-applicable, or stale, retaining original
  outcomes when freshness changes.
- **Obligation coverage:** supported, contradicted, or unresolved within the
  declared scope, with contributing evidence, assumptions, and remaining gaps.
  The LLD may name these `MET`, `NOT MET`, and `UNKNOWN`, but must retain the
  qualified meaning; no state claims universal correctness.

Successful execution does not necessarily support an obligation: a negative
control can execute correctly while demonstrating a prohibited behavior. A
passing model check does not establish model-to-code or business-to-model
correspondence. Agreement among artifacts generated from one interpretation is
weaker than independently grounded challenge. Preserve conflicting results;
never select only favorable evidence or overwrite machine results with prose.
Do not reduce heterogeneous evidence to an unsupported confidence percentage.

### 7. Change impact and coordinated repair

Track dependencies on source passages, interpretations, models and assumptions,
specs, mappings, tests, code, policy, tools, and relevant execution configuration.
Changes invalidate affected evidence and approvals according to their recorded
scope. Preserve demonstrably unaffected evidence; use a conservative fallback
and disclose selection limits when impact cannot be established. Claim digests
alone do not justify retaining evidence after a relevant environment change.

Search within declared scope for new source clauses and implementation paths,
not only changes to existing links. Combine structural checks with semantic
review where omissions cannot be detected mechanically.

Classify findings by the correction needed: implementation violates established
intent; a model/spec/mapping is inaccurate; authority or interpretation is
unresolved; or evidence is missing, stale, insufficient, or unreliable. Preserve
both finding type and execution/freshness state instead of collapsing them into
a single generic failure with one repair action.

Repairs follow separate authorization, intent clarification where needed,
canonical record update, downstream cascade, and affected-check reruns. Preserve
the original discrepancy and decision history. A failed finding cannot be cleared
by changing a prompt, weakening a test, revising a model to match code, or choosing
a more favorable verifier. An approved change of meaning can legitimately revise
those artifacts, but must retain source support and fresh evidence.

Interrupted work resumes from recorded input identities and completed check
results after revalidating freshness, approval, authorization, and remaining
budget. Budget exhaustion and infrastructure failure remain incomplete or
unresolved outcomes, never passes. Do not replay repair mutations merely because
an audit or check retries.

### 8. Access and adoption boundaries

Use project/account authorization and tenant boundaries for sources, evidence,
reviews, and tools. Execute untrusted project code in existing isolated execution
environments. Apply least privilege, secret redaction, and access checks to
reports and evidence links. Portable evaluations use synthetic or sanitized
sources. Supplied source snapshots are valid inputs; automatic ingestion is
optional. Read sources and issue comments only through Paid's trust policies.

Start with documented commands, existing knowledge and artifact services, and
a bounded manual audit. Dedicated dashboards, universal prose compilers,
workflow engines, automatic source ingestion, broad adapters, and recurring
scheduling are not prerequisites. RDR-075 may later schedule a bounded audit,
but recurrence is not owned by this RDR and never authorizes repair implicitly.

## Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Decision and rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Verify PR readiness as the primary unit | Rejected: misses ongoing drift and obligations spanning multiple changes. PRs remain useful evidence and repair vehicles. |
| Rank business text, models, LID, tests, and code by artifact type | Rejected: confuses derivation/granularity with approval and authority; users establish scoped authority. Code remains observed behavior. |
| Require models, LID, all nine layers, or two checks per obligation | Rejected: excludes valid projects and substitutes check counts for risk-appropriate sufficiency. |
| Build separate review-package and rubric add-ons | Rejected: both are parts of this capability and must share authority and evidence records. |
| Create a parallel obligation document as the new source of truth | Rejected: reference and update existing intent; use CIR/OKF records only where appropriate and approved. |
| Repair automatically when an audit finds drift | Rejected: investigation and mutation require distinct authorization. |
| Keep checks independent without correspondence claims | Insufficient: green checks within representations leave the original drift problem unresolved. |

## Trade-offs and Consequences

The design supports incremental adoption and preserves existing intent systems.
It exposes disagreements that local checks miss and keeps human decisions
available to later work. Reusing CIR/OKF avoids an unnecessary new format.

Costs include maintaining mappings and approval provenance, evidence retention,
review effort, check latency, and conservative invalidation. Semantic checks can
miss problems or invent findings; controlled evaluation and source-grounded
review are essential. User clarification can block repair, deliberately avoiding
fabricated authority. Repository intent persistence and Paid mirrors introduce
consistency work that export alone does not solve.

## Rollout Guard

This revision is documentation-only and introduces no runtime flag or schema.
The described implementation requires a default-off
`layered_verification_add_on` entry in `FeatureFlags::DEFINITIONS`, paired with
runtime checks through `FeatureFlags.enabled?(:layered_verification_add_on,
project:)` in the same foundation implementation change.

The **enablement surface** is the existing tenant feature settings, with explicit
project selection on audit requests. The LLD must verify actual setting scope
and access rather than promise unsupported project-level overrides. Enablement
permits the new audit capability; it never grants repair authority or waives a
host policy. Every user or agent invocation must traverse the same guard and
authorization boundary.

Rollback disables new add-on scheduling, preserves evidence and findings, and
leaves in-flight work explicitly incomplete if it cannot safely finish. Existing
CI, security, review, branch protection, and approved-intent conformance continue
unchanged. Flag removal requires closeout evidence, a safe-default decision, and
migration of any remaining configuration. No competing coordinator flag is
introduced from an older draft.

### Phase 1: Intent and audit foundation

Define scoped authority, obligations referencing canonical intent, CIR/OKF
repository persistence and approval provenance, audit-only permissions, common
check/evidence contracts, and the rollout guard. Reuse knowledge, approval,
execution, artifact, and authorization services. Do not require a new obligation
CRUD system or a dedicated table before the persistence choice is justified.

### Phase 2: First usable bounded audit

Accept selected sources and existing artifacts, establish scope and uncertainty,
propose risk-appropriate checks, execute supported commands and correspondence
reviews, and produce both review views with source-grounded findings. Include
business-to-representation checks and model-to-code correspondence when the
project uses formal models. Report unsupported checks and incomplete results.
Expose the project audit action and equivalent project-scoped chat/MCP request,
audit-run progress and history, and Inbox details/actions for clarification,
missing inputs, and findings. Include deduplication, deferral history, current
revision checks, and explicit intent approval/persistence states. This phase does
not start repairs or require every layer to have an adapter.

### Phase 3: Change impact and reusable evidence

Add dependency-aware invalidation, conservative fallback, newly unmapped
material detection, revision-bound packages, resumption, and cost reporting.
Demonstrate a second audit after a source or implementation change without
rebuilding the entire mapping or rerunning demonstrably unaffected checks.

### Phase 4: Separately authorized repair

Support bounded repair requests linked to findings. Record user clarifications
in canonical intent before cascading changes. Integrate normal review and
mandatory host checks; preserve original failures and rerun affected checks.
Expose the separate Inbox repair-request action with scope and authorization
review, create a linked repair run, and reuse existing review lanes. Audit
completion must never enqueue this phase implicitly.

### Phase 5: Design and bootstrap paths

Reuse the same records and contracts for new designs and sparsely documented
implementations. Keep inferred behavior separate from authorized intent. Add
optional adapters and surfaces only where concrete evaluation justifies them.

### Closeout

Follow [the closeout checklist](closeout-checklist.md). Audit each acceptance
criterion against shipped code, tests, docs, and controlled evaluation evidence.
Record required gaps as focused issues; distinguish the audit-first milestone
from completion of all entry paths and authorized repair. Keep the implementation
umbrella open until required work and final acceptance resolve. Update this RDR
and the index to the evidence-backed status, not an issue-count inference.

## Validation

### Acceptance criteria

| ID | Required demonstration |
| --- | --- |
| V1 | A bounded audit produces source-linked obligations, scoped authority/approval states, selected checks, and current coverage; absent authority remains explicit. |
| V2 | A project using formal models detects a seeded model/code discrepancy even while model checks and existing unit tests pass; the report identifies the missing correspondence evidence. |
| V3 | A project without formal models can audit business intent through suitable specs, scenarios, tests, and review without requiring a model or every layer. |
| V4 | A seeded business/model or business/spec contradiction produces a source-grounded finding or explicit uncertainty; green checks do not settle authority. |
| V5 | User clarification is approved and persisted in the existing intent framework, or an approved CIR/OKF repository record, before downstream repair; LID is offered when no framework exists. |
| V6 | Plans vary appropriately for materially different failure modes, explain omissions and residual gaps, permit a justified single-layer plan, and cannot waive host policies. |
| V7 | A business reviewer can validate workflows, rules, and scenarios separately from implementation coverage; approvals bind a specific revision and scope. |
| V8 | Source, model, code, and relevant environment changes invalidate affected evidence; unaffected evidence is reused only with a defensible dependency basis and uncertainty triggers conservative fallback. |
| V9 | A newly introduced obligation or implementation path within declared scope is reported as unmapped, including material with no prior link. |
| V10 | Assertion failures, execution errors, stale evidence, incomplete/not-run checks, non-applicability, and unresolved intent remain distinguishable through synthesis. |
| V11 | An audit can finish with findings without changing maintained project artifacts or starting repair; intent approval alone does not authorize repair. |
| V12 | A separately authorized repair corrects affected artifacts through normal review, records ambiguous decisions first, preserves original failure evidence, and reruns relevant checks. |
| V13 | Deliberately incorrect code fails its designated behavioral check for the intended reason; changing a prompt or weakening an artifact cannot silently clear the finding. |
| V14 | A later audit reuses valid mappings/evidence; interruption, head movement, tool failure, or budget exhaustion cannot produce a false pass or replay an unauthorized repair. |
| V15 | Design and bootstrap use the same authority/evidence contracts; reconstructed code behavior remains distinct from approved requirements. |
| V16 | The default-off guard, source/evidence access controls, tenant isolation, secret handling, and mandatory host checks hold across UI and agent entry points. |
| V17 | Human-actionable findings appear in the Inbox with source/evidence context, a specific decision, consequences, and appropriate actions; routine progress and clean reports stay in audit history. |
| V18 | Chat, acknowledgment, and deferral do not resolve findings; approved clarification must be persisted in canonical intent before it authorizes downstream repair, with intermediate states visible. |
| V19 | Repeated audits update an existing unresolved scoped finding where appropriate; stale actions and repeated repair submissions cannot approve obsolete revisions or launch duplicate repairs. |
| V20 | A project action and project-scoped chat/MCP can start an issue-less bounded audit run with visible scope, revision, budget, provenance, progress, and cancellation; requesting repair creates a separately authorized linked run. |

These IDs are local review references, not allocated EARS spec IDs. V1–V4,
V6–V7, V17–V19, and the audit-only portions of V10–V11 and V20 form the
first usable audit milestone; full closeout requires all criteria and their associated phases.

### Representative evaluation fixtures

1. **Formal-model project:** an ownership protocol has a checked TLA+ model
   and passing unit tests, but code permits duplicate ownership. Exercise
   business-to-model and model-to-code correspondence, optionally through LID
   specs/tests. A second variant places the error in the model, preventing
   automatic preference for formal notation.
2. **Project without models:** an eligibility rule conflicts with an approved
   boundary scenario while ordinary tests pass. Use decision tables, boundary
   or property tests, and selected challenge to expose the mismatch.
3. **Ambiguous authority:** approved sources disagree about controlled handoff.
   The audit retains uncertainty. After explicit clarification and a separate
   repair request, update canonical intent and affected representations.
4. **Change and omission:** revise one source clause, change an environment
   assumption, and add an unmapped code path. Verify targeted invalidation,
   conservative fallback, new-path detection, and preservation of unrelated
   evidence where justified.
5. **Operation separation:** finish an audit with actionable findings and
   approve an intent clarification; verify no repair starts until separately
   requested. Interrupt a later repair and ensure resumption rechecks scope.
6. **Inbox lifecycle:** successive audits find the same ownership discrepancy.
   Show one actionable item with both observations and source context. Chat and
   deferral preserve the finding; a clarification approval shows pending
   canonical persistence when needed. A stale action cannot resolve the item,
   and repeated repair requests cannot create duplicate runs.

Use known expected findings, deliberate negative controls, independent review,
and synthetic or sanitized inputs. Record missed and unsupported findings,
repeatability limits, execution cost, and human review effort. Agent-driven
checks cannot be evaluated solely by their own self-assessment. Later acceptance
also exercises design and bootstrap, not just the initial audit path.

## Open LLD Decisions

- Canonical-source identification and approval/supersession representation for
  repository CIR/OKF records and Paid mirrors, including concurrent revisions.
- Obligation identity, evidence persistence, dependency granularity, retention,
  and conservative invalidation boundaries; avoid parallel maintained intent.
- Concrete check/result schemas, tool discovery, replay requirements, supported
  initial TLA+ command integration, and agent review contracts.
- Exact agent-run goals, Inbox entry kinds, project action placement, forms,
  and chat/MCP contracts implementing the specified user flow; preserve existing
  Inbox/review ownership and equivalent actions through one policy boundary.
- Scoped finding identity and deduplication, deferred-item retrieval, concurrent
  approval handling, and duplicate-run admission rules.
- Measurable evaluation thresholds and a representative workload sufficient to
  justify broader defaults without claiming universal correctness.

Reconsider the design if model/code correspondence cannot be evaluated beyond
artifact linkage, meaningful risk differences do not change useful check plans,
or subsequent changes require rebuilding orchestration. Any proposal for a new
service or maintained representation must justify why existing Paid capabilities
and project intent records cannot serve the requirement.

# RDR-076: Extended Verification Add-On (Defense in Depth) — Business Review Package and Rubric

> Extends RDR-075. This document defines the business-facing review package and
> verification-selection rubric; it does not replace the host policies,
> obligation model, coordinator contract, or evidence requirements proposed by
> RDR-075.

## Decision summary

Adopt a business-facing review package for every verification plan produced by
the layered-verification coordinator. The package makes the business obligation,
selected verification layers, evidence, approvals, unresolved questions, and
completion decision reviewable by a person who did not author the change.

The package is available through three entry paths:

1. **Shared design path** — a business obligation is introduced or changed in
   an approved design before implementation begins.
2. **Independent audit path** — a reviewer starts from an existing obligation,
   implementation, or release decision and asks whether the evidence is still
   sufficient.
3. **Bootstrap/implementation path** — an implementation or change request
   enters verification without a complete review package and must be brought
   to a reviewable state before completion.

RDR-075 supplies the layered-verification foundation: typed obligations,
selection, coordinator scheduling, claim-addressed evidence, obligation-level
coverage, finding classes, and these same three entry paths. RDR-076 adds the
business review surface and expands the selection decision from six categories
to nine complementary layers. It is defense in depth: a layer may strengthen a
decision, but it may not silently waive a mandatory host policy or convert
missing evidence into success.

## 1. Shared design entry path — obligation-first business review

This path is the default for a new feature, a material behavior change, or a
change to an existing business obligation. It begins before implementation and
leaves an approved package that later paths can reuse.

### 1.1 Business-facing review package

The package is a versioned, immutable-at-review artifact with links to its
source claims and evidence. It should be readable without opening source code.
Its minimum contents are:

| Package part | Required content | Business question answered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Obligation register | Obligation ID, actor, desired outcome, constraints, priority, owner, and status | What must be true, for whom, and who owns the decision? |
| Change boundary | In-scope behavior, explicit non-goals, affected surfaces, and assumptions | What is changing and what is deliberately unchanged? |
| Verification plan | Selected layers, rationale, required evidence, order, and stop conditions | How will we know the obligation is satisfied? |
| Approval record | Approver identity/role, decision, timestamp, package digest, and conditions | Who accepted the plan and under what conditions? |
| Evidence index | Claim digests, producer, environment, timestamps, validity window, and links | Can another reviewer find and trust the proof? |
| Coverage report | `MET`, `NOT MET`, or `UNKNOWN` per obligation, with confidence and rationale | Which obligations are proven, disproven, or unresolved? |
| Findings and exceptions | Finding class, severity, owner, due date, disposition, and compensating control | What remains risky and what happens next? |
| Completion decision | Release/merge decision, reviewer, rationale, and package digest | Is this review complete, and is the result permitted to proceed? |

The package is complete only when every in-scope obligation has a terminal
coverage state, every required approval is recorded, all mandatory layers have
valid evidence, and no unresolved item is being represented as `MET`.

### 1.2 Workflow and handoffs

| Order | Actor | Trigger and action | Handoff / approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Business owner | Creates or changes an obligation and names the outcome, constraints, and acceptance boundary | Hands obligation to product/design owner |
| 2 | Product/design owner | Confirms scope, actors, non-goals, and decision authority | Approval that the obligation is ready for planning |
| 3 | Verification coordinator | Normalizes the obligation, identifies affected claims, and proposes layers using the rubric | Hands a draft plan to engineering and an independent reviewer |
| 4 | Engineering owner | Translates obligations into design elements, tests, conformance checks, and implementation seams | Confirms the plan is implementable; does not approve its own evidence as independent audit |
| 5 | Evidence producers | Execute planned checks and record immutable evidence linked to claim digests | Handoff to coordinator for synthesis |
| 6 | Independent reviewer | Reviews package completeness, layer rationale, exceptions, and evidence provenance | Approves, rejects, or requests clarification |
| 7 | Business owner / release authority | Decides whether the remaining risk is acceptable within policy | Records final completion or an explicit exception |

The ordering is intentional: formalize the business meaning before selecting
tests, translate it before implementing, and audit the assembled evidence only
after the evidence-producing work has run. The coordinator may schedule work in
parallel after the obligation and plan are approved, but it may not skip a
predecessor's required artifact.

### 1.3 Decision rules

1. An obligation must have one stable identity and a content digest. Textually
   similar obligations are not merged merely because they use the same words.
2. A selected layer must name the property it verifies, its expected evidence,
   its owner, and its invalidation conditions.
3. Mandatory host policies remain mandatory. A successful add-on check cannot
   waive CI, security, code review, branch protection, or approved-design
   conformance.
4. `MET` requires current, claim-linked evidence from every required layer.
   `NOT MET` means evidence contradicts the obligation. `UNKNOWN` means a
   required check did not run, is stale, is inconclusive, or lacks provenance.
5. A failed verification finding cannot be cleared by rerunning a different
   prompt or by changing the selected verifier without an explicit plan
   revision and fresh approval.
6. A layer may be omitted only when the coordinator records a justified
   inapplicability decision. Cost, schedule pressure, or reviewer preference
   alone is not sufficient justification.
7. An exception requires an owner, expiry/review date, scope, compensating
   control, and approval from the authority that owns the affected risk.
8. Evidence is invalid when its input digest, environment, policy snapshot, or
   validity window cannot be established. Invalid evidence produces `UNKNOWN`.

### 1.4 Acceptance scenarios

| Class | Scenario | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permitted | A payment-limit obligation selects behavioral tests, mutation testing, implementation conformance, and an independent semantic audit; all evidence is current and linked | Package may reach `MET` and proceed through the normal release approval |
| Permitted | A UI accessibility obligation omits formal verification because the coordinator records it as inapplicable to the chosen runtime representation | Omission is permitted when the rationale and stronger applicable layers are approved |
| Forbidden | A green unit-test run is used to declare a security or authorization obligation satisfied without policy/conformance evidence | Coverage remains `UNKNOWN` or `NOT MET`; completion is blocked |
| Forbidden | An engineer marks a failed verification as complete by changing the prompt, verifier, or claim wording | Original finding remains; plan revision and independent review are required |
| Boundary | A low-risk copy change affects no executable behavior but changes a customer-facing obligation description | Linkage/freshness and independent semantic review may suffice; coordinator records why deeper layers are unnecessary |
| Boundary | A database migration changes a limit-enforcing query and also changes its business wording | Treat as a material obligation change: formalize, reselect layers, and invalidate stale evidence |
| Unresolved | A required external integration is unavailable and the test cannot establish the result | Coverage is `UNKNOWN`, with a recovery owner and retry/exception path; it is not `MET` |
| Unresolved | Mutation testing survives a mutant because the expected behavior is ambiguous | Open a clarification finding, revise the obligation/test contract, and rerun affected layers |

### 1.5 Exceptional paths and recovery

- **Missing owner or approver**: pause at the coordinator, assign the missing
  role, and do not schedule a completion decision.
- **Plan changes after approval**: increment the plan revision, calculate
  impacted claims, invalidate affected evidence, and obtain approval again.
- **Stale or corrupted evidence**: quarantine the record, preserve the original
  failure metadata, rerun the producer, and retain the audit trail.
- **Transient infrastructure failure**: classify the failure, retry through the
  owning runner's bounded retry policy, and report `UNKNOWN` while unresolved.
- **Contradictory evidence**: retain both records, create a conflict finding,
  and require independent semantic adjudication; never select the favorable
  result by default.
- **Emergency release**: use the existing emergency authority only with an
  explicit exception, compensating control, expiry, and post-release review.

## 2. Independent audit entry path — evidence and decision review

This path starts with an implementation, release, incident, customer report,
or periodic review rather than a newly authored design. It is deliberately
independent of the original author so that the package can detect a plausible
but incomplete verification story.

### 2.1 Audit workflow

1. The auditor identifies the affected obligation set and records the source
   revision, environment, and review question.
2. The coordinator reconstructs or imports the last approved package and
   compares obligation, implementation, policy, and evidence digests.
3. The auditor checks layer selection against the rubric below, including
   whether a materially different obligation received a materially different
   plan.
4. Evidence producers rerun only the invalidated or explicitly sampled checks;
   the auditor must not rewrite the business obligation to fit observed code.
5. The coordinator synthesizes coverage and findings. The independent auditor
   approves the decision, rejects it, or records `UNKNOWN` with a recovery plan.

### 2.2 Verification-selection rubric

The coordinator selects layers by obligation property, not by ticket label,
file type, or preferred tool. Multiple layers are expected where they answer
different failure modes.

| Business obligation / risk | Most appropriate layer(s) | Why these layers are selected | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The business outcome, actor, scope, and non-goals are unambiguous | Business-process formalization; independent semantic audit | Formalization makes the obligation reviewable; an independent reader detects language that is technically precise but business-wrong | Approved obligation record, ambiguity decisions, reviewer sign-off |
| The verification plan covers the obligation and has an appropriate order | Verification planning; independent semantic audit | Planning checks completeness and dependencies; audit checks that the plan is not mechanically complete but substantively weak | Versioned plan, layer rationale, dependency graph, audit decision |
| The obligation is represented in the approved architecture and implementation seams | Engineering translation; implementation/conformance | Translation maps business language to design/code boundaries; conformance detects implementation outside the approved design | Design links, typed claims, conformance report, changed-surface map |
| Evidence still refers to the current obligation, code, policy, and environment | Linkage/freshness; change impact detection within the coordinator | Digest and freshness checks catch stale proof; impact analysis selects what must be rerun after change | Claim/content digests, validity window, invalidation report |
| User-visible behavior satisfies the acceptance outcome | Behavioral testing; independent semantic audit | Tests execute observable paths; an independent audit checks that the path actually represents the business outcome | Passing scenario results, boundary/error cases, screenshots or API assertions |
| A safety, invariant, or state property must hold for all modeled cases | Formal verification; implementation/conformance | Formal methods cover modeled state space where examples are insufficient; conformance checks connect the model to the shipped boundary | Model/checker result, assumptions, proof artifact, conformance mapping |
| Tests must detect a plausible incorrect implementation | Mutation testing; behavioral testing | Mutation survivors reveal weak assertions; behavioral tests provide the expected contract against which mutants are evaluated | Mutation score, survivor disposition, test results |
| The shipped artifact implements the approved design and policy | Implementation/conformance; linkage/freshness | Conformance checks structural and policy boundaries; freshness proves the checked artifact is the one being released | Build/revision digest, conformance report, release manifest |
| The complete evidence story is semantically credible and risks are acceptable | Independent semantic audit; all applicable producer layers | The audit is the final cross-layer check; it cannot substitute for missing producer evidence | Signed audit checklist, findings disposition, final coverage report |

Selection rules:

- Always select business-process formalization for a new or materially changed
  obligation.
- Always select linkage/freshness when evidence or implementation is reused
  across revisions.
- Select behavioral testing for an observable outcome unless the obligation is
  explicitly non-behavioral and the omission is justified.
- Select formal verification when the obligation contains an invariant,
  authorization rule, state-machine property, or exhaustive safety claim that
  can be modeled.
- Select mutation testing when the risk is that tests appear green while not
  checking the business rule, especially for limits, permissions, and failure
  handling.
- Select independent semantic audit for release-critical, high-impact, or
  disputed obligations, and whenever the coordinator detects contradictory or
  surprising evidence.

### 2.3 Audit acceptance and recovery

The audit completes only when the package digest, selected-layer rationale,
coverage statuses, findings, and final decision are all recorded. A failed audit
creates a finding rather than silently reopening implementation work. Recovery
is to classify the gap, assign its owner, invalidate dependent evidence, and
return to the earliest entry path that can correct the intent: design for an
ambiguous obligation, planning for a missing layer, engineering translation for
an unrepresented rule, or implementation for a failed check.

## 3. Bootstrap/implementation entry path — making incomplete work reviewable

This path supports existing work that predates the package or arrives with
partial intent. It is an adoption path, not a lower standard. The coordinator
must make uncertainty visible and progressively close the gap before claiming
completion.

### 3.1 Coordinator role and layer interfaces

The coordinator is a mechanically simple control-plane component. It owns
identity, ordering, policy enforcement, scheduling, state transitions, digests,
and evidence storage. It does not decide what business text means by matching
keywords or by treating a large change as inherently risky; semantic judgments
are explicit inputs from the business owner, reviewer, or a structured verifier.

Each layer has the same boundary contract:

| Interface field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Input | Obligation/claim digest, source revision, policy snapshot, and relevant prior evidence |
| Output | Structured result, status, producer identity, environment, timestamps, assumptions, and evidence references |
| Failure | Typed outcome classified as `NOT_MET`, `UNKNOWN`, retryable, or non-retryable; no swallowed errors |
| Invalidation | Declared inputs whose change makes the result stale |
| Review | Human or automated approval role required before the result can affect completion |

The coordinator schedules the layers through the existing verification/run
infrastructure proposed by RDR-075. It must preserve mandatory host gates and
must not expose arbitrary implementation or prompt control as a substitute for
the typed interface.

### 3.2 Evidence recording and change impact

Every evidence record binds at least:

- obligation and claim content digests;
- package and plan revision;
- source/release revision and relevant artifact digest;
- policy/configuration snapshot;
- producer, verifier version, environment, and execution time;
- result, assumptions, limitations, and linked findings; and
- retention, expiry, and re-run requirements.

On a change, the coordinator computes impact from these bindings and the
obligation-to-design, design-to-implementation, and implementation-to-evidence
links. It invalidates only what is affected when the dependency graph supports
that precision, but it may conservatively invalidate a larger set when impact
cannot be established. A changed business obligation always invalidates its
prior approval and dependent evidence; a documentation-only change may preserve
behavioral evidence when linkage/freshness proves that the executable and
policy inputs are unchanged.

Concrete example: changing `maximum refund is $500` to `$750` invalidates the
obligation digest, design translation, boundary tests, mutation results, and
final audit. It should not be treated as a copy edit merely because the code
diff is one line. Conversely, correcting a spelling error in a non-semantic
description can retain execution evidence if the package records and approves
the unchanged semantic digest.

### 3.3 Adoption principles

1. **Start with the obligation.** Import existing requirements, then ask a
   business owner to confirm them before generating a plan.
2. **Make gaps first-class.** Use `UNKNOWN` and typed findings during migration;
   never manufacture green evidence to satisfy a rollout metric.
3. **Prefer small, reversible slices.** Adopt one capability or obligation
   family, measure stale evidence and unresolved findings, then expand.
4. **Keep layers replaceable.** A layer's contract and evidence shape are more
   durable than a particular test runner, model, or vendor.
5. **Preserve independence.** The author may explain evidence, but the final
   semantic audit belongs to a reviewer who can reject it.
6. **Do not weaken existing controls.** The add-on remains unavailable or
   non-blocking until its rollout guard, host-policy boundaries, and evidence
   behavior are validated as specified by RDR-075.
7. **Record the reason for every exception.** Exceptions expire and return to
   review; they are not a permanent alternate path.

### 3.4 Bootstrap completion conditions

Bootstrap work is complete when the imported obligation set is owner-approved,
the selected plan is reviewed, each applicable layer has run or has an approved
inapplicability decision, evidence is claim-linked and fresh, and unresolved
findings have an explicit disposition. A package may be operationally adopted
with `UNKNOWN` findings only when the governing authority records an exception;
it may not report full completion or silently satisfy a blocking host policy.
